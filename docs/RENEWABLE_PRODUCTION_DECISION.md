# F3 owner decision: rights over cultivated crops

Status: design proposal only; no production implemented. F0–F2 evidence is sufficient
to proceed beyond finite deposits, but the existing ownership contract only covers
open-access natural deposits, personally gathered goods and communal job cargo.
It does not settle ownership of crops created through earlier labor.

The roadmap explicitly reserves property-rights/cooperation decisions for the owner.
This choice changes incentives to invest labor, return after growth, and harvest
another person's work. It must not be hidden in a utility coefficient or scheduler.

## Common minimal model, independent of the rights choice

A physically located fertile plot has bounded simultaneous labor access. Recorded
work and growing time create a harvest opportunity; harvest becomes physical,
age-tagged food. Natural production is an explicit material source/transform ledger,
not unaccounted `food += population`. Workers travel, reserve actual labor capacity,
work, potentially leave, return and carry output. The same clock controls work,
growth, hunger, sleep and spoilage. Food age begins at the defined physical harvest
transition, never resets through handling. Initial finite-stock experiments remain
unchanged. No tools, housing, trade, prices or worker quotas.

Before implementation, register a small fixed range of labor/yield/growth values
and land locations, with failed/no-labor/no-land controls and a research feasibility
witness. Do not search for parameters that force sustainability. Autonomous agents
may decline cultivation or fail; material, locality and exclusivity gates still hold.

## Rights options for the owner

A. Open-access land AND unharvested crops. Only active work reserves a plot. Food
ownership begins when harvested: personal normally, communal under an accepted
communal undertaking. Closest continuation of existing gathering rights, but labor
investors can lose the later harvest opportunity to others.

B. Open-access land, cultivator-owned growing crop. Working does not own the land;
the planted crop/output belongs to the cultivating agent (or commons for a communal
undertaking). This introduces a durable claim on the product of earlier labor.

C. Communal cultivated plots and output. All crop/output is Commons-owned, even for
voluntary cultivation without a delivery job. This extends the communal institution
into production and changes the reward/allocation assumption.

No option is selected automatically. The implementation outline is reviewable, but
coding production before this answer would decide property rights implicitly.
