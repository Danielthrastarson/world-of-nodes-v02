Your command vocabulary

Bot practice: choose Play vs bot in the lobby, then Start practice. The bot commands East through the same observation and order rules. It pushes the bridge and uses the ford; its plans remain hidden. Surrender and Play again reset the match. Practice uses the normal twenty-minute clock and sixty-second reconnect grace.

Compact controls: select a formation to reveal its five command buttons at the bottom of the field. Hover a command for its execution details. The bar tucks away while you choose a target, then returns after your click. The Command desk holds Orders, People, Works, and Reports. Details beside a selection opens its duties. Clicking an outpost or hill site opens Works. Esc cancels targeting, closes the desk, or clears selection; it never cancels an already dispatched order.

Quick controls: click a named formation button or double-click a troop. Right-click the map to MOVE. The regular MOVE command also moves generals; for scouts and carriers it sets their duty rendezvous. Scroll over the field to zoom; Fit map restores the full field. Auto command chooses a nearby command position or a source with a recently observed courier. Delivery estimates use last reported positions, not hidden current positions.

1–5 activate pinned instructions. Select people, press a key, then click the map. Shift-click adds route waypoints for MOVE. Ctrl-drag assigns an area for HOLD, OBSERVE or ENGAGE. Esc cancels target selection.

Alt+1–5 recalls troop groups. Ctrl+Alt+1–5 assigns the current selection. Drag to select; Shift adds. The group buttons provide the same controls; Ctrl-click a group button to assign it. Initial groups are line, ranged, light, couriers, and carriers.

Each general provides live local vision. Staffed outposts need an unobstructed signal path. Hollow, faded contacts are old reports, not current locations. Dots with a dark ring denote ranged infantry; diamonds denote generals. Static hatching marks the exact woods cells, and the survey grid is 100 metres. Map key explains the symbols. Labels keep the same screen size while zooming. Choosing a camera position reveals nothing.

Nearby generals and headquarters hand orders to the addressed troops. Those troops pass the original instruction to nearby members of the same addressed group, taking 0.3 seconds per local link; separated groups still need a messenger. Couriers can deliver when they encounter their recipients along the assigned route. Receipt counts update only when observations or acknowledgements reach you. Keep couriers near a forward command position. Staffed, connected outposts extend that network; building is not required for ordinary nearby commands. Living people keep physical space, so crowds and bridge queues can delay travel.

Supply carriers repeatedly haul food to their assigned rendezvous. Troops must meet them there. Reserve a carrier for each forward outpost: its staff also need food. Construction uses materials carried from headquarters and actual worker time. Select workers near headquarters, then click a hill site and Build.

Capture the enemy headquarters with five combatants and no defenders for thirty seconds. The match draws at twenty minutes. General replacement costs an existing person, ten food and one minute.

Try moving your light formation toward the bridge, keeping another force near the ford, and watching how your opponent responds. Your two scouts begin observing the approaches automatically.

Five private command slots

One intention, one manner, and either a follow-up or a fallback. Opponents see behavior, never these slots.

Columns: slot, intention, manner, after, command key, group key (Alt).

Fallback plans use the last point you set with “Set fallback,” or your headquarters if unset.
